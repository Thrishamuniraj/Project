Article Title

Choosing the right promotional product starts with a simple question: what will the recipient actually use? A pen that sits in a drawer does less for your brand than a tumbler that rides along to work every morning. Before browsing a catalogue, define the goal — awareness, retention, or a thank-you — and let that steer the category.

Match the Product to the Moment

A conference giveaway and an executive gift call for different products entirely. Trade-show attendees respond well to lightweight, useful items — tech accessories and tote bags travel easily. Executive gifts lean toward fewer, higher-quality pieces: a premium tumbler or a leather-accented notebook says more than a stack of branded pens.

Team apparel works best when it's something people would wear even without the logo. A well-cut polo or a soft fleece pullover gets worn long after the event; a stiff, boxy tee ends up in the donation pile.

Budget Per Recipient, Not Per Order

It's tempting to set one total budget and divide by headcount, but working backward from a target per-recipient cost — say $8 for a team order or $35 for a client gift — keeps quality consistent as your list grows or shrinks.

Ask About Minimum Order Quantities Early

MOQs vary widely by product and branding method. Embroidered items often carry a higher minimum than screen-printed ones because of setup time. If your headcount is close to a supplier's MOQ, ask whether a small overage is available — reordering a handful of units later usually costs more per piece than padding the original order.
