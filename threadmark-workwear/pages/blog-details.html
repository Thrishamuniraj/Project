How to Get Uniform Sizing Right the First Time

By Sofia Kowalski·March 3, 2026·6 min read

In This Article

  1. Why Sizing Mistakes Are So Expensive
  2. Collecting Sizes Without Guesswork
  3. Choosing the Right Size Run
  4. Building a Reorder Profile

Nothing undermines a new uniform rollout faster than sizing that doesn't fit. A shirt that's too tight across the shoulders or a jacket that runs three sizes large makes an otherwise professional-looking team appear disorganized — and it's almost always avoidable.

Why Sizing Mistakes Are So Expensive

Every exchange costs more than the fabric. There's the shipping cost, the delay in getting the correct size to the employee, and — for embroidered garments — the risk that the replacement doesn't get embroidered before the employee's first shift. On a 50-piece order, even a 10% exchange rate means five garments moving twice.

"We used to budget for a 15% reorder rate on every rollout. Once we started collecting real measurements instead of guessed sizes, that dropped under 3%." — Sofia Kowalski, Quality & Fabric Specialist

Collecting Sizes Without Guesswork

The most reliable approach is a simple size-collection form sent directly to each employee, rather than relying on a manager's estimate. Ask for:

  • Standard size (XS–4XL) based on the employee's usual fit in similar garments
  • Preferred fit — tailored, relaxed or true-to-size
  • Any known fit issues with previous uniform orders

For safety-critical garments like flame-resistant workwear, we recommend an in-person fit check rather than self-reported sizing.

Choosing the Right Size Run

A "size run" is the distribution of sizes you order — for example, ordering more Medium and Large than XS or 4XL. Rather than ordering evenly across all sizes, use your roster's actual size breakdown. Most corporate teams see a size run weighted toward S–XL, while industrial and construction crews often skew toward L–3XL.

Building a Reorder Profile

Once your initial order ships correctly, save that sizing data against your company account. New hires can then be matched to a similar role's typical size, and replacement garments ship without re-collecting measurements every time.

Need help setting up a size collection form for your team? Our uniform specialists can walk you through it as part of your first bulk order.
